How they differ where it matters

A is the serious one — it treats training as a document you accumulate, and it puts hours and dollars in front of you immediately. Best if the people you're losing are the ones who think this is out of reach.

B is about the sensation of the thing, and it's the only one that would work with real cockpit photography layered in. Best if you're competing with other schools on feeling like the more professional operation.

C is social proof as the entire structure. It only works if you'll give me real names, real dates, and a photo of the actual wall — invented ones would read as invented.

D is the most reassuring of the six. A checklist is a promise that nothing is improvised, which is exactly what someone nervous about small aircraft needs to hear. Best if the objection you're overcoming is fear rather than price.

E is the insider's direction — it opens on coded weather and trusts the reader to want to be let in. It's also the only one that could run on live data, so the page would say something different every day. Best for a self-selecting, technical audience.

F is the loudest and the simplest, and it would survive being printed on a banner or a t-shirt. Best if you need one image people remember and repeat.

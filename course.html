What you learn

Two halves: the job you will do on board, and the day you have to get through to be offered it.

In the cabin

  • Safety and emergency procedures
  • Door and slide operation, armed and disarmed
  • Evacuation commands
  • Fire, smoke and oxygen equipment
  • First aid and in-flight medical basics
  • Galley, trolley and meal service
  • Difficult passengers, delays and diversions
  • Crew roles, rosters, layovers and fatigue

For the assessment

  • A CV that clears the first screen
  • Application photographs, taken to standard
  • Aviation English and cabin announcements
  • Grooming, uniform and the reach test
  • Group exercises
  • Competency interview answers
  • A full mock assessment day, with written feedback

The assessment day

Airlines generally run it in this order, and candidates leave at every stage. We prepare you for each one.

  1. 1

    CV screen

    Often sifted by software before anyone reads it.

  2. 2

    English

    Written, then spoken. Usually the largest cut.

  3. 3

    Group task

    With strangers, while assessors take notes.

  4. 4

    Interview

    Competency questions, under time pressure.

  5. 5

    Final checks

    Reach, swim, medical and documents.
